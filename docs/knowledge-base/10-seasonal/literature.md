## 1. Halloween / Sell in May

### Bouman-Jacobsen 2002

**Paper**: Bouman, S., Jacobsen, B. (2002). *The Halloween Indicator: Everywhere and All the Time*. American Economic Review.

- **Winter returns (Nov-Apr)** vs **summer returns (May-Oct)**.
- **36 of 37 countries** show higher winter returns.
- **Magnitude**: ~4.2% higher winter vs summer.
- **Robust**: 323 years of data (50+ countries, multiple centuries).
- **Strategy**: long Nov 1, flat May 1.
- **Edge maintained OOS**: tested post-2002, still strong.

### Update 2020

- 62,962 monthly observations over 323 years.
- Halloween effect prevails in 89/114 countries.
- 42 statistically significant, only 1 opposite.
- Strong in developed markets + emerging.

## 2. January effect

### Keim 1983

**Paper**: Keim, D. (1983). *Size-related Anomalies and Stock Return Seasonality*. Journal of Financial Economics.

- **Small-cap stocks** outperform in January, especially first 2 weeks.
- **Magnitude**: ~2-3% small-cap outperformance vs large-cap.
- **Cause**: tax-loss selling late Dec → reinvested early Jan.
- **Decayed post-1980s**: widely known, arbitrage eroded.
- **Still measurable**: ~0.5-1% in recent years.

### Reinganum 1983

- January effect persists across small caps but is concentrated in micro caps.
- Trade costs reduce net edge.

## 3. Santa Claus rally

### Stock Trader's Almanac (Yale Hirsch)

- **Definition**: last 5 trading days of December + first 2 trading days of January.
- **S&P 500 average**: +1.3% in 7 days since 1950.
- **Frequency**: positive 76% of time (since 1950), 79% in some measures.
- **Why**: anticipation of January effect + lighter volume + short seller vacation.
- **Limitation**: weeks prior to Christmas do NOT show elevated returns (no whole December effect).

## 4. Turn of month effect

### Hensel-Ziemba 1987

**Paper**: Hensel, C., Ziemba, W. (1987). *Investment results from playing the turn of the year*.

- **TOM (turn of month)**: last 4 trading days of month + first 3 trading days of next month.
- **Concentrated returns**: TOM (7 days) ~50% of total monthly returns.
- **DJIA evidence**: TOM +0.473%, total monthly +0.349% → ROM negative.
- **Mechanism**: salary + 401k + institutional inflows concentrated in TOM.
- **Strategy**: long SPY only in TOM, flat otherwise.

### Cadsby-Prince 1998 update

- TOM effect persists globally, more concentrated in Japan + UK.

## 5. Day of week effect

### French 1980

**Paper**: French, K. (1980). *Stock Returns and the Weekend Effect*.

- **Monday lower returns**: often negative.
- **Friday higher returns**: positive bias.
- **Cause**: settlement delays (3-day settlement pre-1995), short sellers cover Friday.
- **Decayed post-1990s**: T+2 settlement, widely known.

### Cross 1975

- Original weekend effect documentation.
- Friday close → Monday close negative on average.

## 6. Presidential cycle

### periodic 4-year US cycle

- **Year 3 (pre-election)**: highest average returns (~+15-20%).
- **Year 1 (post-election)**: lowest (~+0-5%).
- **Cause**: incumbent party stimulates economy before election.
- **Maintained**: 1948-2020, 18 cycles observed.
- **Limitation**: small sample (18 cycles).

## 7. Holiday effect

### Ariel 1990

- Pre-holiday returns higher than normal days.
- Day before Thanksgiving, July 4th, Christmas → +0.3-0.5% average.
- Lighter volume + positive sentiment.

## 8. Cap summary

**Edge maintained**:
- Halloween / Sell in May (Bouman-Jacobsen 2002) — robust globally 323y.
- Turn of month (Hensel-Ziemba 1987) — concentration of returns.
- Santa Claus rally — 76% positive since 1950.

**Edge decayed**:
- January effect (post-1980s).
- Day of week (post-1990s).

**Edge too small sample**:
- Presidential cycle (18 cycles only).
- Holiday effect (marginal).
